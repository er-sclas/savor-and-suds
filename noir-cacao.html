§ The Story
C-002

The bar that bites back.

Criollo accounts for less than 0.01% of world cacao production. We work exclusively with a pair of Trinitario estates in Ecuador — fermented seven days on banana leaf, sun-dried, stone-ground.

The roasted husk — typically discarded in chocolate production — is the secret. Micro-milled to 180 microns, it delivers true mechanical exfoliation without the synthetic microbeads most exfoliants rely on. A by-product, reclaimed.

§ The Tasting Journey
Three Stages

Dark. Mineral.
Quiet.

i.

First Taste

Bittersweet.
Smoke.
Tobacco.

Reads like 70% dark eaten at dusk. Faint wood-smoke from the oak salt. The bitterness is not aggressive — it is clarifying, the way good espresso is clarifying.

ii.

On Skin

Gritty.
Exfoliating.
Earnest.

The husk catches. Work slowly. The bar is more chisel than cream. Knees, elbows, the hollow behind the heels — places that benefit from friction.

iii.

After Rinse

Polished.
Mineral.
Still.

Skin reads as buffed, not bruised. The sillage is almost dry — tobacco-adjacent, with a trace of woodsmoke. Appropriate for evenings; faintly medicinal in the best sense.

§ Suggested Ritual

Evening. Low light.
A closed door.

Noir Cacao is a night bar. The bitterness reads differently under low light, the way a digestif reads differently from a morning coffee. Taste first — a small grain, bitter then mineral.

Then lather in warm (not hot) water, applied with deliberate pressure. Do not rush. The husk does the work of a washcloth. Recommended before bed; sillage of woodsmoke pairs well with cold cotton sheets.
